## 1. Testing generative vs contrastive (Q1 and W1)

We performed an ablation where we change the loss for a contrastive learning (infoNCE) one, with the following logic:

**Physics (galaxy) encoder:**
- *positive pairs in physics space*: the anchor and **the same source seen by the other survey**
- *negative pairs*: for each anchor, the cross-survey counterparts of all **other** galaxies in the batch.

**Instrument encoder:**
- *positive pairs in instrument space*: the anchor's own  **same-instrument sky neighbours**.
- *negative pairs*: every **other** anchor's neighbours in the batch.

SimCLR-style projection heads are used in the loss only, and the probes read the raw encoder outputs, following Audenaert et al 2025.

Everything else is held constant. There is one asymmetry that favours the contrastive baseline: it encodes the anchor image itself during training while our model never does. Both the contrastive model and our control **exclude the 5,469 downstream-evaluation galaxies from SSL training**. We find the following:


### 1.1 Reconstruction

We train a counterfactual generative flow-matching decoder on top of the
embeddings generated by the contrastive learning encoder, following the same triplet logic from our method, thus ablating what the encoder learned to capture as a result of its training objective.

Results on 512 held-out validation galaxies:

| conditioning | MSE HSC | MSE Legacy | MSE all |
|---|---|---|---|
| ours  | 0.0474 ± 0.0031 | 0.1610 ± 0.0047 | 0.1042 |
| frozen contrastive embeddings | 0.0679 ± 0.0045 | 0.1936 ± 0.0071 | 0.1308 |
| **relative MSE increase** | **+43.1%** | **+20.3%** | **+25.5%** |

We believe this is caused because contrastive learning does not necessarily enforce the model to encode the information that is relevant for image reconstruction, specially of the survey that has higher resolution (HSC). Overall, our model provides better reconstruction and counterfactual view generation.

## 2. Amount of data needed to train the architecture and rare phenomena detectability

We thank the reviewer for pushing on the data-scale question, which was raised in Q2, Q4, as a weakness and the Limitations. We ran the ablation; the result varies between the two latents, and we think it strengthens the paper.

**What we varied, and why.** In multi-instrument astronomy the scarce resource is not single-survey data, it is the *overlap*. A new survey may hold millions of galaxies while crossmatch between surveys might yield many orders of magnitude fewer sources. With our architecture, only the anchor path requires a crossmatch, while the same-instrument conditioning neighbours need no counterpart in the other survey. Concretely, the model in the paper trains on ~100k crossmatched anchors but draws its neighbours from a pool of 468k sources. We therefore ablate **the number of crossmatched anchors**, holding per-survey coverage at full density (meaning that we still access the
pool of 468k sources when looking for sky neighbors to form instrument pairs for each target): the realistic deployment scenario for a new survey.

**Setup.** Four nested variants trained on 1,000 ⊂ 3,162 ⊂ 10,000 ⊂ 31,622 crossmatches
(1.1% / 3.4% / 10.7% / 33.9% of the training anchors), each trained for the same 75k steps
as a full-data control. 

**Result 1:** Downstream R² on 5,469 held-out crossmatches, using the same probes as
Table 5. The `untrained` row is the random-init encoder:

| N crossmatches | mean physics (7) | mean Legacy instr. (5) | mean HSC instr. (2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 0.593 | 0.289 | 0.219 |
| 3,162 | 0.612 | 0.390 | 0.282 |
| 10,000 | 0.625 | 0.471 | 0.353 |
| 31,622 | 0.634 | 0.499 | 0.375 |
| full (93k training, step-matched control) | 0.640 | 0.611 | 0.478 |
| full (paper checkpoint) | 0.639 | 0.591 | 0.492 |
| untrained (random-init floor) | 0.394 | 0.135 | 0.119 |

Measured as the fraction of attainable R² recovered (full-data control minus random-init
floor), **1,000 crossmatches — 1% of our training set — already recover 81% of the physics
signal but only 28–32% of the instrument signal**; by 31,622 the physics latent is at 98%
while the instrument latents are still at 71–77% and climbing.


**Practical answer to "when does this become applicable to a new survey?"** For
morphology-driven science questions, and for cross-survey retrieval, ~10³ crossmatches
suffice. For accurate instrument-property inference and for noise-model applications,
performance improves when scaling up to ~10⁵.

**Result 2 — reconstruction.** Reconstruction MSE on counterfactual image
generation/reconstruction on 256 held-out anchors: HSC improves monotonically with N
(0.107 → 0.053), i.e. more crossmatches buy sharper generation for the higher-resolution
target. Legacy shows an overall trend of improving with number of anchors but it is not
fully monotonic.

| N crossmatches | distinct images seen | MSE HSC | MSE Legacy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 6,942 | 0.1069 ± 0.0147 | 0.1752 ± 0.0099 |
| 3,162 | 21,487 | 0.0746 ± 0.0084 | 0.1538 ± 0.0063 |
| 10,000 | 64,210 | 0.0591 ± 0.0058 | 0.1611 ± 0.0062 |
| 31,622 | 176,610 | 0.0534 ± 0.0055 | 0.1876 ± 0.0072 |
| 99,000 (full data control) | 443,027 | 0.0729 ± 0.0104 | 0.1809 ± 0.0074 |

The distinction images seen column is how many unique images after including those that were seen as instrument sky neighbors.

**Result 3 — rare-object retrieval (Q2).** We repeated the Fig. 5 lens query for every training-size variant and counted how many promising lens candidates appear among the top 8 nearest neighbors in the physics latent space. Panels from all variants were pooled in random order and scored by eye, blind to which model produced each one.

Crucially, the Fig. 5 query lens, no model ever saw it as a training target. In the **physics** space, at only N=1,000, the model returns 6 lens-like objects in the top 8. In the **instrument** space, both the paper model and the N=31,622 variant return **0/8**. These two results are the significant ones: with only 1,000 cross-matches, and with the lens absent from training, the physics latent still can perform the task, while the instrument space returning 0 lenses serves as a sanity check.

**Limitation we will add.** This ablation varies the *overlap* at fixed single-survey size; it does not independently vary per-instrument catalog size. The instrument-property scaling may therefore reflect the number of distinct instrument realizations seen as much as the number of crossmatches (for N=1,000, there are 6,942 distinc images seen in total during training and for N=31,622 it is 176,610). We will state this, and we note that the physics result is robust to this confound in the useful direction: near-ceiling physics R² is reached having touched fewer than 7,000 distinct images in total.

## 5. Finding phenomena in HSC that do not appear in Legacy

We do not have this result, but the machinery in the paper points at a concrete way to do it, and we will add it to the discussion. Our
counterfactual decoder predicts the HSC view of a source from its Legacy view. Ranking sources by the residual of the predicted HSC image from the *real* is a good metric to find objects whose appearance is not recoverable from the shallower, lower-resolution survey.
