## 1. Testing generative vs contrastive (Q1 and W1)

We replace our loss with InfoNCE. For the physics encoder, positives are cross-survey views of the same source and negatives are other galaxies' counterparts. For the instrument encoder, positives are same-instrument sky neighbours and negatives are other anchors' neighbours. Following Audenaert et al. (2025), SimCLR projection heads are used for the loss.

Everything else is fixed. One asymmetry favours the contrastive baseline: it encodes the anchor during training, while our model never does. Both models exclude the 5,469 downstream-evaluation galaxies from SSL training.


### 1.1 Reconstruction

We train a counterfactual generative flow-matching decoder on top of the embeddings generated by the contrastive learning encoder, following the same triplet logic from our method, thus ablating what the encoder learned to capture as a result of its training objective.

Results on 512 held-out validation galaxies:

| conditioning | MSE HSC | MSE Legacy | MSE all |
|---|---|---|---|
| ours  | 0.0474 ± 0.0031 | 0.1610 ± 0.0047 | 0.1042 |
| frozen contrastive embeddings | 0.0679 ± 0.0045 | 0.1936 ± 0.0071 | 0.1308 |
| **relative MSE increase** | **+43.1%** | **+20.3%** | **+25.5%** |

Contrastive learning does not enforce encoding reconstruction-relevant information, this is particularly noticeable for the higher-resolution HSC. Our model therefore provides better reconstruction and counterfactual generation.

## 2. Amount of data needed to train the architecture and rare phenomena detectability

We thank the reviewer for pushing on the data-scale question. In multi-instrument astronomy, the scarce resource is not single-survey data, it is the *overlap*. With our architecture, only the anchor path requires a crossmatch, while the same-instrument conditioning neighbours need no counterpart in the other survey. Concretely, the model in the paper trains on ~100k crossmatched anchors but draws its neighbours from a pool of 468k sources. We ablate **the number of crossmatched anchors**, holding per-survey coverage at full density of 468k sources: the realistic deployment scenario for a new survey.

**Setup.** Four nested variants trained on 1,000 ⊂ 3,162 ⊂ 10,000 ⊂ 31,622 crossmatches

**Result 1:** Downstream R² on 5,469 held-out galaxies:

| N crossmatches | mean physics (7) | mean Legacy instr. (5) | mean HSC instr. (2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 0.593 | 0.289 | 0.219 |
| 3,162 | 0.612 | 0.390 | 0.282 |
| 10,000 | 0.625 | 0.471 | 0.353 |
| 31,622 | 0.634 | 0.499 | 0.375 |
| full (93k training, step-matched control) | 0.640 | 0.611 | 0.478 |
| full (paper checkpoint) | 0.639 | 0.591 | 0.492 |
| untrained (random-init encoder) | 0.394 | 0.135 | 0.119 |

As a fraction of attainable R² (full-data control minus random-init floor), **1,000 crossmatches—1% of training—recover 81% of the physics signal but only 28–32% of the instrument signal**. At 31,622, these reach 98% and 71–77%, respectively. Thus, ~10³ crossmatches suffice for morphology and retrieval, while instrument/noise applications continue improving toward ~10⁵.

**Result 2 — reconstruction.** On 256 held-out anchors, HSC reconstruction improves monotonically with N, while Legacy improves overall but not monotonically.

| N crossmatches | distinct images seen | MSE HSC | MSE Legacy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 6,942 | 0.1069 ± 0.0147 | 0.1752 ± 0.0099 |
| 3,162 | 21,487 | 0.0746 ± 0.0084 | 0.1538 ± 0.0063 |
| 10,000 | 64,210 | 0.0591 ± 0.0058 | 0.1611 ± 0.0062 |
| 31,622 | 176,610 | 0.0534 ± 0.0055 | 0.1876 ± 0.0072 |
| 99,000 (full data control) | 443,027 | 0.0729 ± 0.0104 | 0.1809 ± 0.0074 |

“Distinct images” includes those seen only as instrument neighbours.

**Result 3 — rare-object retrieval (Q2).** We repeated the Fig. 5 lens query for each variant and blindly scored pooled top-8 panels. The query lens was never a training target. At N=1,000, the **physics** space returns 6/8 lens-like objects, while the paper and N=31,622 **instrument** spaces return **0/8**. Thus, retrieval works with only 1,000 crossmatches, and the instrument result confirms it is physics-driven.

**Limitation we will add.** This ablation varies the *overlap* at fixed single-survey size; it does not independently vary per-instrument catalog size. The instrument-property scaling may therefore reflect the number of distinct instrument realizations seen as much as the number of crossmatches (for N=1,000, there are 6,942 distinc images seen in total during training and for N=31,622 it is 176,610). We will state this, and we note that the physics result is robust to this confound in the useful direction: near-ceiling physics R² is reached having touched fewer than 7,000 distinct images in total.
