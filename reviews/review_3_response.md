We are grateful for the reviewer's time, but we believe parts of this review describe a different submission. Our method is a dual-encoder architecture with a conditional flow-matching decoder, not a CVAE with a shared encoder/decoder; we use neither a consistency regularizer nor Pearson-correlation weighting; and we work with real data — a cross-match of ~104,000 galaxies from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys and HSC, with all downstream metrics on held-out crossmatches — rather than synthetic benchmarks with controlled SNR. We do not analyze gravitational-wave data. We address the points that we believe do apply to our submission, as we think they are good ones.

*Correlated artifacts across instruments (Limitation 2).*
We thank the reviewer for highlighting the omitted discussion regarding failure modes when measurement artifacts are correlated across different sensors. Our proposed dual-encoder architecture fundamentally relies upon the assumption that observing the same physical system with different instruments introduces uncorrelated noise and distinct instrument-specific distortions. This is an insightful concern that we will address by revising the camera-ready version to appropriately state this underlying assumption.

*Performance under sensor that share little information (Limitation 1).*
This is a real operating boundary and we will state it explicitly. Our physics/instrument decomposition is possible only to the extent that the two views carry shared information about the source: as that mutual information vanishes, the physics latent has nothing to encode and the model reduces to two independent per-instrument autoencoders. We would expect this failure to be diagnosable by the physics downstream performance collapsing to the untrained case (R² 0.639 trained vs 0.394 for an untrained encoder) while instrument-latent probes would be unaffected. However, we cannot test with this dataset the extreme regime of two instruments sharing almost no source information. We will add both the assumption and this scope limit to the limitations section.
